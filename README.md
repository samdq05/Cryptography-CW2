# Cryptography Coursework

This repository contains a Jupyter Notebook completed for **MTH3026 Cryptography** at the University of Exeter.

The project applies number theory and finite field theory to computational problems in cryptography, with a particular focus on polynomial arithmetic over finite fields and algorithms for solving the discrete logarithm problem.

## Contents

The notebook covers:

- Irreducibility of polynomials over finite fields
- Polynomial arithmetic and inverses in finite fields
- Arithmetic in finite fields of characteristic 2
- Testing whether elements generate the multiplicative group of a finite field
- The **Baby-Step Giant-Step (BSGS)** algorithm for solving discrete logarithms
- The **Pohlig-Hellman** algorithm for solving discrete logarithms
- Prime and prime-power factorisation
- The Chinese Remainder Theorem
- Computational comparisons of discrete logarithm algorithms
- Analysis of algorithmic efficiency and running time

## Finite Fields and Polynomial Arithmetic

The first part of the coursework investigates polynomials over finite fields, including methods for determining irreducibility.

For polynomials over $\mathbb{F}_2$, the notebook implements tests based on the relationship between irreducible polynomials and expressions of the form

$$
X^{2^n}-X.
$$

The implementation is then used to identify irreducible polynomials and construct finite fields using irreducible polynomial moduli.

## Generators of Finite Fields

The notebook also investigates generators of the multiplicative group of a finite field.

A generator is identified by testing whether its powers satisfy the appropriate conditions associated with the prime factors of the group order.

This is then used to construct examples where discrete logarithms can be computed within finite fields.

## Discrete Logarithms

### Baby-Step Giant-Step

The **Baby-Step Giant-Step** algorithm is implemented to solve problems of the form

$$
g^x = h,
$$

where $g$ and $h$ are elements of the same finite field.

The implementation uses the standard decomposition of the exponent into baby and giant steps, giving a time complexity of approximately

$$
O(\sqrt{N}),
$$

where $N$ is the order of the group generated by $g$.

A version of the algorithm is also implemented for subgroups of a specified order, which is required as part of the Pohlig-Hellman algorithm.

### Pohlig-Hellman

The **Pohlig-Hellman algorithm** is implemented for groups whose order has a factorisation into relatively small prime powers.

The algorithm reduces the original discrete logarithm problem to a collection of smaller problems in subgroups and combines the resulting congruences using the **Chinese Remainder Theorem**.

The notebook also compares the computational cost of Baby-Step Giant-Step and Pohlig-Hellman for a group of order

$$
N = 2^{64}-1.
$$

This illustrates how the factorisation of the group order can have a substantial effect on the efficiency of discrete logarithm algorithms.
